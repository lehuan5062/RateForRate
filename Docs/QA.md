## Trailer (`Data/Trailer.csv`, `Source/ShiftHappens/Trailer/`, `Tools/trailer/`)

The trailer is **one scripted run of the game itself**, not an edit of several recordings. A director (`FShiftTrailer`, compiled into the editor and Development builds, out of the Shipping build) plays `Data/Trailer.csv`: it runs a clock in bars of the music (112.5 BPM, 1 bar = 2.1333 s = 128 frames at 60 fps; the 8-bar loop restarts at bars 0, 8 and 16, which are the drops), starts fresh runs under black or white flashes, flips the real night (the game's own sound and music crossfade), pins the music's tension layer, glides the scripted cursor, spotlights part of the screen, zooms in (the board camera and the HUD together) and draws the trailer text with Slate (the "SHIFT split" slams and the lower-left captions, `PaintTrailer` in `UI/SShiftHud.cpp`). The music and the effects are the game's own and play live, so picture and sound are one performance and the music stays the same across day and night. The cut is 23 bars (49.1 s) plus a 0.4 s fade to black. Raw recordings and outputs are git-ignored (`Saved/Trailer`); the cue file, the director and the scripts are in the repo.

* **The cue file** has the columns `Id,Bar,Cmd,Args` (ASCII, no commas in the text) and is read when the run starts, so timing, places, warp values and wording change without a rebuild. The commands (`black`, `flash`, `title`, `run`, `warp`, `bot`, `speed`, `night`, `tension`, `click`, `hover`, `spot`, `zoom`, `slam`, `caption`, `end`) are listed at the top of `Trailer/ShiftTrailer.h`; coordinates are the design pixels of the sim (1280 x 720); a `run` names jam, seed, bot, the sim seconds to skip and the speed (the skipped time is how the DQ banner or the results card is made to land on a bar line).
* **Play it** in the editor (Play, then `Shift.Trailer` in the console) or standalone, full screen or windowed: `Tools\run.ps1 -Visible -Sound -ResX 1920 -ResY 1080 -Extra -ShiftTrailer`. It quits itself at the end cue.
* **Author it silently**: `Tools\trailer\preview.ps1 -Bars 0.5,8,16.05` (or `-Seconds 1,5,9`) plays the run headless (no window, no sound; with no music clock to wait for, film time is game time) and tiles a screenshot per film time, with the time on each tile, into `Saved\Trailer\auth\sheet.png` (`-Name`, `-Cols`, `-TileWidth`, `-Cues` change the output and the cue file). A frame is 1/60 s of game time, so the screenshots are deterministic: what the sheet shows at a time is what the recording shows there.
* **Record it** (`shoot.ps1`, with `setup_obs.ps1` making the OBS profile and collection `ShiftTrailer`: window capture of the Development package's window (`Tools\package.ps1`, `Saved\PackageDev`), the game's own audio only via application audio capture, AMD AMF H.265 at 1080p60 and 12 Mbps VBR_LAT, started and stopped through OBS's local WebSocket server; the H.264 encoder at 40 Mbps skipped 1-2% of the frames as encoding lag while the game used the same GPU, H.265 at 12 Mbps still skips about 1%, and `shoot.ps1` prints OBS's count after each recording). It first records a 10 s calibration run (`Tools/trailer/calibrate.csv`: a white flash on each of the first four bar lines); `measure.py` compares the flashes with the beats of the sound in that recording, which gives `-ShiftTrailerLatency` (how long the picture waits for the sound; every cue moves by it); then it records the trailer with that latency. Each run's log ends with `Trailer: the end (clock .. s = .. bars, wall .. s)`: the two agree while no frame is longer than the engine's 0.4 s limit, which is what keeps the picture locked to the music.
* **Finalize it** (`finalize.ps1`): finds the music's first beat in the recording, cuts 23 bars + 0.4 s starting on the video frame at or before it, brings the sound to -14 LUFS with one static gain and a -2 dB limiter (fade out over the last 0.6 s), encodes 1080p60 H.264 (CRF 19, to stay under about 30 MB) + AAC to `Saved/Trailer/Dist/trailer45.mp4`, and writes `trailer45_poster.png` next to it and a contact sheet to `Saved/Trailer/review/`. It prints the checks: size, frame rate, duration, integrated loudness, true peak. (`measure.py` needs Python with numpy.)
* **The shoot can run while you work.** It records the packaged game (no editor overhead; build it first with `Tools\package.ps1`; rebuild it after a code or content change; use `pwsh` for `finalize.ps1`, Windows PowerShell 5.1 treats ffmpeg's messages as errors). It injects nothing: no key presses, no mouse movement (OBS is driven through its WebSocket server on a local port with a random password; its own settings are put back afterwards). The game window is sent to the back of the window stack as soon as it is on screen (primary monitor; OBS's Windows Graphics Capture records covered windows), and the game plays quietly (`-Volume`, 25% by default; `finalize.ps1` normalizes the loudness anyway). Muting it in Windows is not possible: that silences OBS's application audio capture too, and Windows remembers a mute per program, so the script clears it. The engine mutes an unfocused game by default; the trailer director turns that off (`FApp::SetUnfocusedVolumeMultiplier`). Expect a second of flicker when the window appears. A busy PC can make the game hitch: `shoot.ps1` compares the game clock with wall time against the calibration run and warns when the take slipped more than 50 ms. Your own Unreal Editor can stay open; OBS must not be running. `Saved\Trailer\STOP` ends it after the current run.
* **Sound**: the only audio is the game's own MetaSound music and effects, recorded by OBS application audio capture (the game process only, no other app). No `.wav` is made, the game records nothing and no recorder is part of it; the recordings stay in `Saved/Trailer/raw` (`.mkv`) and the final MP4.

## What has been checked (and how)

* **Sim core**: determinism and invariants in the standalone harness; the same results from the in-engine balance commandlet (identical table on two runs with the same seeds).
* **Alerts** (round 5): a sock-bot run logs every alert (`Alert: t=<sim s> kind=<n> '<title>' / '<detail>'`; kinds 1 strike, 2 Blame offer, 3 Blame taken, 4 disqualification, 5 final push, 6 final audit) and the screenshots of `-ShiftBot=sock -ShiftJam=1 -ShiftSeed=3 -ShiftWarp=<s> -ShiftSet=BotBlame=0` at 1280 x 720 and 1920 x 1080, day and night, show strike 1 (t 3.1 s), strike 2 (19.7 s), the offer (`BlameMaxFake=0.5`, 39.6 s), the refusal and the disqualification beat (38 s warp, 2.5 s later), the blame taken (`BotBlame=1,BlameMaxFake=0.5`) and the results screen that follows 1.8 s after a disqualification. A pause freezes the alert, the countdown and the sound, and B / the bar do nothing while paused.
* **Recordings** (round 12): a jam played with scripted clicks (`-ShiftRecord -ShiftSpeed=6 -ShiftClicks=...`, 30 clicks that flipped, weakened, were gated, locked and reinforced) writes a 3 KB file; `Tools\replay.ps1` replays it with the StateTrees and ends in the same state hash and result (`MATCH`), and so does `-ShiftReplay` in the game itself (`Replay: finished ...
